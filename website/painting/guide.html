From sprue to table

Painting guide

Ten steps, tick them off in order – after step 5 the lance is ready to play. Details on every technique: painting techniques.

Factions Guide Techniques Scheme Paints

What you need

Brushes

Size 1 for areas, size 0 for details, an old frayed one for drybrushing

Primer

Black and white from a spray can, or an airbrush

Paints

6–8 pots are enough: your three faction colours, black, white, one metal, one wash

Tools

Side cutters, hobby knife, fine file

Wet palette

Home-made from a food container, kitchen paper and baking paper – keeps paint open for hours

Light

A bright daylight lamp does more than any expensive paint

  1. 01

    Preparing

    Cut the parts off the sprue (don’t snap them), scrape mould lines away with the back of the blade. Give Catalyst plastic models a quick wash in lukewarm soapy water to remove the release agent. Gluing: plastic cement for plastic, superglue for metal.

    Glue first, prime second – with models this small, painting in sub-assemblies is almost never worth it.

  2. 02

    Priming – zenithal

    First completely black from below and at an angle, then white or light grey only from above at an angle. Result: light and shadow are already in the model before you touch the first paint.

    On 6 mm models this is the single biggest leap in quality. Thin coats from 20–30 cm away, better three times. → Technique

  3. 03

    Applying the base colour

    Main colour on 60–70 % of the surface. Thin the paint with a drop of water and apply two to three thin coats instead of one thick one. The fast alternative: contrast or speed paint straight over the zenithal primer – that way a lance is done in an evening.

    Rule: light colour first, dark trim over it afterwards. A dark line on light is easier to draw than the other way round. → Technique

  4. 04

    Trim and panels

    Second colour on about 20–30 % – shoulders, hips, one side of a leg, weapon housings. Stick to clearly delineated armour plates, then it looks like engineering and not like paint.

  5. 05

    Washing / shade

    Thinned Nuln Oil or Agrax Earthshade into the recesses, panel lines and joints. On small models work precisely with the tip of the brush, don’t flood the whole model.

    Lift off excess immediately with a clean, dry brush. → Technique

  6. 06

    Edges and drybrush

    ’Mechs are almost nothing but edges – that is their great advantage. Drybrush a lighter version of the base colour over the whole model, then trace individual edges with an even lighter tone.

    Really wipe the brush dry until hardly any paint comes off. → Technique

  7. 07

    Details

    Cockpit glass: dark blue or green, a smaller lighter arc on top, then a tiny white dot at the top left. Weapons and joints: Leadbelcher with Nuln Oil. Sensors and warning lights: pure red without a wash.

    The cockpit is the eye-catcher on these models. If only one detail turns out clean, make it this one. → Technique

  8. 08

    Markings

    Faction crests, lance numbers and hazard stripes. Waterslide decals are far cleaner than freehand at 6 mm – with softener (Micro Set / Micro Sol) they settle into the armour edges. Stripes work well with narrow masking tape. → Technique

  9. 09

    Weathering

    Dab chips on with a piece of kitchen sponge and dark brown-grey, dust on the legs with a drybrush, soot at the muzzles. Stay sparing: more than three effects tip over into diorama territory. → Technique

  10. 10

    Basing and sealing

    Texture the base, rim colour to match the gaming mat, grass tufts or gravel. Finally two thin coats of matt varnish – these models get handled constantly in play.

    On Iron Wind metal models, gloss varnish under the matt varnish is worth it – it holds far better. → Technique

Four rules that make the difference at 6 mm

Three colours, no more

60–70 % main colour, 20–30 % trim, under 10 % accent. Anything beyond that looks busy at table distance.

Contrast beats subtlety

The models are 25–40 mm tall. Soft transitions vanish, hard edges and clear blocks of colour stay visible.

Paint by the lance

Four models through every step at once. Same colour, one brush load, used four times.

Play first, perfect later

Primer plus base colour plus wash already looks complete on the table. You can add everything else at any time.

Schedule: the first lance

  1. Assemble and clean up four models
  2. Prime black, then lighten with grey from above
  3. Faction colour in two thin coats
  4. Trim colour on shoulders and hips
  5. Wash into the recesses, lift off the excess
  6. Drybrush with the lightened base colour
  7. Cockpit, weapons, sensors
  8. Bases and matt varnish
