Thinning paint & wet palette half the battle

Almost every “beginner mistake” on small models is really unthinned paint: it fills panel lines, leaves brush strokes and drowns details. The goal is the consistency of milk – the paint flows off the brush but still covers.

Procedure

  • Never paint straight from the pot onto the model – always onto the palette first.
  • Work in a drop of water (the tip of the brush is enough), test: if the paint runs into a panel line without flooding it, it is right.
  • Better two to three thin coats than one thick one. Each coat must be dry before the next (at 6 mm: 1–2 minutes).

Building your own wet palette

  • Flat food container, two layers of kitchen paper, let them soak up water.
  • Lay a piece of baking paper flat on top – mix on that. The paint stays open for hours and is gently moistened from below.
  • Lid on: mixes survive until the next evening – important when half the lance is still waiting for the same panel colour.

Typical mistakes

  • Too much water: the paint pulls into the recesses and dries blotchy. Then mix in a little fresh paint.
  • Mixing on a dry plastic palette: after five minutes the consistency turns – exactly why you want the wet palette.
Priming & zenithal biggest leap in quality

The primer gives the paint grip – and the zenithal prime builds light and shadow into the model before the first real colour lands. On models 25–40 mm tall this is the single biggest leap in quality there is.

Procedure

  • Degrease the models (lukewarm soapy water), let them dry, stick them onto a painting handle or cork.
  • Black from 20–30 cm away from all sides, including from below – thin misted coats, no wet film.
  • White or light grey only from above at an angle (about 45°), like the midday sun. Top surfaces become light, undersides stay dark.
  • If you have an airbrush, you spray finer; two spray cans do the job just as well.

Typical mistakes

  • Too close or too much: the primer goes grainy or runs. Several thin passes, let it dry in between.
  • Spraying outside in high humidity: the can spits grains of dust. A dry day, or indoors with ventilation.
  • White from all sides: then the zenithal effect is gone – the white belongs on top only.
Base colour & layering 60–70 % of the surface

The faction’s main colour goes onto most of the model – thin enough for the zenithal shading to show through. Then you lighten selectively (layering): each further coat a little lighter, on an ever smaller area.

Procedure

  • Thinned base colour in two to three coats on 60–70 % of the surface (paint sequence per faction).
  • First highlight (base colour + a little white, or the next lighter tone) only on top surfaces and the centres of large areas.
  • Second highlight only on the highest points – at 6 mm a single highlight step plus edge highlight is often enough.
  • Rule: light colour first, dark trim after – a dark line on light is easier to draw than the other way round.

Typical mistakes

  • Opaque coats over the whole surface: that paints over the zenithal effect – leave the shadows underneath alone.
  • Yellow, red or white straight onto black: covers terribly. Light grey or bone as an intermediate layer first (see the faction tips).
Washes / shades depth in the recesses

A wash is heavily thinned, free-flowing paint that runs into recesses and dries dark there: panel lines, joints, grilles. On ’Mechs with their many armour plates a targeted wash works like an outline around every panel.

Procedure

  • Standard: Nuln Oil (neutral, for grey/blue/metal) or Agrax Earthshade (warm, for earth and sand tones).
  • At 6 mm let it run precisely with the brush tip into lines and joints – don’t flood the whole model (that is the “dip look” and it swallows all contrast).
  • Lift pools on open surfaces immediately with a clean, dry brush (the brush wicks like a fuse).
  • Let it dry completely (10–15 minutes) before carrying on.

Typical mistakes

  • Wash as a substitute for shadows everywhere: just makes the model dirty. Recesses yes, surfaces no.
  • Fretting over wash edges: simply layer the base colour over dried edges (“coffee stains”) on surfaces.
Drybrushing edges in seconds

When drybrushing, almost no paint is left in the brush – only the raised edges catch it. ’Mechs are almost nothing but edges: that is why this technique delivers enormous effect for enormously little effort at 6 mm.

Procedure

  • Dab an old, frayed brush (or a make-up brush) into lightened base colour.
  • Wipe it on kitchen paper until almost nothing comes off – only then start.
  • Quick, light strokes across the surface detail – the paint only catches on edges and raised areas.
  • Better twice very lightly than once heavily; then trace individual edges with an even lighter tone.

Typical mistakes

  • Too much paint in the brush: gives “frosty” surfaces. Really wipe it dry.
  • Using a good detail brush: drybrushing ruins brush tips – that is what the old one is for.
Edge highlights the finishing touch

Edge highlighting is the controlled sister of drybrushing: a light line exactly on the armour edge, painted with the side of the brush tip. At table distance this is precisely what makes a model look “finished”.

Procedure

  • Mix a clearly lighter version of the surface colour (or take a ready-made light tone).
  • Lay the brush flat against the edge and draw the edge along the tip – don’t “paint” with the tip, the edge guides the brush.
  • At 6 mm highlight only the upper and outer edges: shoulders, torso top edges, gun barrels, knee armour. Lower edges stay dark.
  • Where two steps are wanted: a broad line in the mid tone, a thinner lightest line only at corners and junctions.

Typical mistakes

  • All edges equally light: looks like a wireframe. Light comes from above.
  • Paint too thick: the line goes crumbly – thin it here too.
Contrast & speed paints a lance in one evening

These paints (Citadel Contrast, Army Painter Speedpaint, Vallejo Xpress) are glaze and shading in one: over a light zenithal prime they take care of base colour, shadow and gradient in a single pass – perfect when a whole lance needs to hit the table fast.

Procedure

  • Prerequisite: a light, clean zenithal prime (contrasts don’t work over black).
  • Apply briskly and in one direction, pick up pools at lower edges – the paint collects in the recesses by itself.
  • Don’t go over half-dry spots twice (gives blotches). Correct gaps only after it has dried through.
  • Then refine as usual: drybrushing or edge highlights on top.

Typical mistakes

  • On large smooth surfaces without detail: contrast lives on recesses – smooth torso plates go blotchy. Better to layer classically there.
  • Trying to thin and layer them like “normal paint” – they are not built for that; use speedpaint medium instead of water.
Metallics weapons, joints, cooling fins

Bare metal sets the technical accents: gun barrels, joints, actuators, cooling fins. Less is more – a completely metallic ’Mech quickly looks unpainted.

Procedure

  • Dark silver (Leadbelcher/Gun Metal) on weapons and joints; metallic paints need hardly any thinning, but a good stir.
  • Nuln Oil over it – metal without a wash looks like a toy.
  • Individual glints with light silver (Stormhost/Shining Silver) on muzzles and edges.
  • Gold/brass only as a decorative colour: edges, trim strips, insignia – sparingly (see the Kurita/Marik schemes).

Typical mistakes

  • Carrying metal particles into the water cup: a separate rinsing glass for metallics, otherwise the white will glitter later.
  • Metal first, surfaces after: painted-over metal particles shimmer through. Metal comes late in the process.
Decals crests & numbers

Faction crests and lance numbers are hard to paint cleanly freehand at 6 mm – waterslide decals are not. With softener they even settle over panel edges.

Procedure

  • Varnish the spot gloss (a dot of gloss varnish is enough) – on matt you get silvering.
  • Cut the decal out closely, 20–30 seconds in lukewarm water, slide it into place on its backing.
  • Apply with Micro Set (blue), correct the position, dab off the water with a paper towel.
  • Micro Sol (red) over it – the decal softens and snuggles down. Don’t touch it until it is dry.
  • Finally seal with matt varnish – the difference in sheen disappears.

Typical mistakes

  • On a rough, matt surface: air under the decal glitters silver. Always gloss underneath first.
  • Pushing a half-softened decal: it tears. Patience after the Micro Sol.
Weathering chipping, dust, soot

A ’Mech is a 60-ton war machine, not a display piece. Three sparing effects tell that story: chipped paint, dust on the legs, soot at the muzzles. More quickly tips over into diorama territory.

Procedure

  • Chipping: dab a piece of kitchen sponge into dark brown-grey (Rhinox Hide), dab it almost dry as with drybrushing, then stipple onto edges, shoulders and footholds. If you like, put a dot of metal into the large chips.
  • Dust: drybrush a light earth tone (the base colour!) from bottom to top – feet heavily, shins fading out.
  • Soot: drybrush black or dark grey very lightly around muzzles and exhausts; alternatively pigment powder.
  • Effects always after the decals, but before the matt varnish.

Typical mistakes

  • Scattering chips evenly over the whole model: wear has logic – edges, grips, treads, not in the middle of a plate.
  • Too many effects: from the fourth effect on, legibility at table distance drops.
Freehand & patterns tartan, animal patterns, stripes

The striking faction schemes live on patterns: Jade Falcon flashes, Smoke Jaguar rosettes, Northwind tartan. At 6 mm the rule is: suggest rather than depict – two or three elements in a prominent spot are enough.

Procedure

  • Stripes: narrow masking tape (2–3 mm), press the edges down firmly, paint thinly over it, peel off the tape, touch up with the surface colour.
  • Tartan: two crossed pairs of lines in red and yellow across the chest plate – and the plaid impression is done (Northwind Highlanders).
  • Animal patterns: irregular ovals in the second colour on shoulders and thighs, a dark ring around each with the finest brush tip (Smoke Jaguar).
  • Scales/flashes: better a few large, clean jags than many small ones – sketch it in pencil on the model first? At 6 mm paint directly and dare to leave gaps.

Typical mistakes

  • Pattern over the whole model: busy and illegible. One carrier element (torso OR shoulders) is enough.
  • Trying to draw long lines with a shaky hand: rest your hand, turn the model, do the line in two short strokes.
Brush care & sealing so it lasts

Brushes

  • Never let paint reach the ferrule (metal sleeve) – it dries there and splays the tip.
  • Rinse in between, stroke back into shape on a paper towel; wash with brush soap after the session.
  • Two good brushes (size 1 and 0) with a real point beat ten cheap ones.

Sealing

  • Gaming models get handled constantly: two thin coats of matt varnish to finish.
  • Metal models (Iron Wind): gloss varnish first (flexible, adheres better), then matt varnish over it.
  • Bring the cockpit glass back to life with a dot of gloss varnish after matting.

Typical mistakes

  • Matt varnish in high humidity: a white haze (“frosting”). Emergency fix: gloss varnish over it immediately, then matt again.
  • One thick coat of varnish instead of two thin ones: collects in the corners.
